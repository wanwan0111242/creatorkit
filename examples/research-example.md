# Example: research

Realistic demo of the skill's output for a small research task. Shows the incremental-save pattern and the final brief.

## Input

> Research how small makers price their digital products before I write a newsletter issue about it.

## Working file created: `research-digital-product-pricing.md`

### Key Facts (appended after round 1)

- Gumroad's own creator data has long reported a large share of sales under $10 for small makers (source: gumroad.com blog archive).
- Several indie makers publicly post "sold my first $X" breakdowns with PWYW / low-price experiments (sources listed below).

### Sources (deduplicated, appended each round)

- https://gumroad.com/blog (pricing/creator data posts)
- https://www.indiehackers.com (product pricing discussions)
- (…)

### Open Questions

- Which specific Gumroad post contains the exact sales-share statistic? (round 1: not yet located)

### Contradictions

- None found yet between sources; flag if later rounds disagree on PWYW vs fixed pricing outcomes.

## Final brief (abridged)

```
# Research Brief: Digital Product Pricing for Small Makers

## Key Facts
- Small makers overwhelmingly launch with low price points (< $10) to reduce
  purchase friction (multi-source, VERIFIED across Gumroad blog + Indie Hackers threads).
- PWYW (pay-what-you-want) is used mainly as a discovery strategy, with a floor
  price to avoid $0 sales (verified; several makers report 5–15% conversion to
  "above-floor" payments — single-source claims, label as such).

## Sources
- https://gumroad.com/blog
- https://www.indiehackers.com/…

## Open Questions
- Exact % of sub-$10 sales on Gumroad: unverified, needs primary source.
- Whether $3 floor vs $9 suggested changes perceived value: no data yet.

## Contradictions
- One maker reports PWYW increased total revenue; another reports it attracted
  free-loaders with no lift. Both stated; no verdict from data yet.
```

## Notes

- Every claim carries a source URL; single-source claims are labeled, not asserted as fact.
- The file was saved after every batch — a session break at any point loses nothing.
- The user still owns the final call on anything marked "Open Questions".
